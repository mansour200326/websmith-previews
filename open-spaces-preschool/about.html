About us

“We took over the centre in 2020 and have since put a lot of aroha and hard mahi into creating a beautiful, warm, welcoming environment for our most precious tamariki to thrive, learn and play in nature.”

Katie Beecroft, owner of Open Spaces with her husband Rob

Our story

A small boutique centre on the outskirts of Whangarei, nestled on a 133 acre farm with a significant area of native forest. Open all year round apart from public holidays.

We're licensed for 30 children: 8 under two years old and the balance over two. Our tamariki spend time in nature with weekly Wild Woods excursions: Puriri trees over 400 years old, friendly Tuna, streams to explore, trees to climb, and a maze of old trails to lead us on adventure and discovery.

Learning experienced in the bush is brought back to the centre, where our connections to Papatūānuku are part of our daily practice. Our practice is influenced by two childcare philosophies: 'children are their own best risk managers', as promoted by Claire Warden of Mindstretchers in Scotland, and, in our Under 2 area, the Magda Gerber (RIE) philosophy.

Our philosophy

Manaaki whenua, manaaki tangata, haere whakamua. Care for the land, care for the people, go forward. Open Spaces is a loving and engaging place of learning for our tamariki, kaiako and whānau.

Kaitiaki / guardiansNature first

  • We emphasise a nature-based education for its breadth of learning
  • We offer meaningful connections to Papatūānuku throughout our curriculum
  • We promote respect, care and protection of all life
  • We want tamariki to know the joy of being with nature

Ngā HonongaRelationships

Respectful, reciprocal, honest and trusting relationships are the foundation for all learning. They build wellbeing, a strong identity, self-esteem and the confidence to climb the tallest mountain.

WhakamanaEmpowerment

Tamariki initiate and lead their own learning, and take on leadership and responsibility. Our emergent curriculum is built on children's strengths and interests.

Whānau TangataFamily and community

We see families and community as experts and partners in nurturing tamariki, and bring family perspectives into the curriculum. We welcome, value and respect diversity.

Te Tiriti and Te WhārikiOur foundations

We acknowledge, respect and promote the principles of Te Tiriti o Waitangi and an environment that values Te Ao Māori. Te Whāriki, the New Zealand early childhood curriculum, guides everything we do as teachers.
