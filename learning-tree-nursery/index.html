A neighbourhood nursery

“Fundamentally, The Learning Tree is a neighbourhood nursery. Like a tree, it will grow from our neighbourhood and shape our neighbourhood.”

From the Learning Tree Philosophy

Our school was set up by educators trained in the British Early Years Foundation Stage. Many of our families are expats who move often, and still want to belong somewhere. So every child is welcomed here as a member of the family.

English is the main language of teaching. Music, Arabic and French are taught by specialists.

The building is the classroom

We call the environment our “third teacher”. Children don’t sit in one room all day: in a typical week they move between themed spaces, which we call the mobile classroom.

  1. 01

    The Learning Square

    The heart of the nursery

    Our central indoor role-play area, full of natural light. The dress-up zone, reading wall, children’s theatre and ICT area are here. It is also where your child is dropped off each morning.

  2. 02

    Expressive Arts and Design Room

    Painting, drawing, drama

    Hands-on art and self-expression. Children paint, draw, act things out and record their own ideas.

  3. 03

    Construction and Number Zone

    Numbers and building

    Numbers, shapes and building things, one of the stops on the week’s route.

  4. 04

    Language Playroom

    Talking, stories, early literacy

    Words, stories and talking together, on the way to early reading and writing.

  5. 05

    The outdoor play area

    Private, on Astroturf, under a shady canopy

    An age-appropriate play gym, sand and water play, and PE games for big movements.

  6. 06

    The Parents’ Lounge

    First floor, next to our offices

    Coffee mornings, reading groups, Mums and Toddlers events and talks on family well-being. It doubles as a gallery of the children’s work.

A day here

  1. Drop-off at the Learning Square

    Each class starts at a slightly different time, so your child gets a calm hello and full attention.

  2. Quiet, focused time

    Carpet conversations and table-top activities, built on specially written lesson plans.

  3. Outdoor play and snack

    High-energy play outside, then snack time. Growing children need both.

  4. Free flow

    Children follow their own interests in the indoor role-play area. Teachers join in and take notes to plan what comes next.

  5. Specialist lessons

    Music, Arabic and French, alongside a strong focus on early numeracy and literacy.

Parents are partners

We work closely with families, and we expect you to be part of your child’s learning. You will always know how your child is doing:

  • The Learning Journal, a record of your child’s progress
  • Regular parent-teacher meetings
  • Weekly newsletters with ideas to support learning at home
  • An open door to your child’s class teacher

The Parents’ Lounge

Upstairs, next to our offices. A meeting place, a quiet corner, and a year-round gallery where the children show you what they have made.
