Classes and curriculum

Four classes, from 2 months up to 4 years. We place children by their stage of development as well as their age, and the age gap in a class is usually no more than 8 months.

01

Infants

From 2 months

1:3one adult to three babies

Our baby programme focuses on care, comfort and trust, alongside explorations of wonder based on the senses. Babies are part of our family group and spend time with the older children too.

Our practitioners get to know each baby: what they like, what they don't, and what interests them, and respect their own schedules. Each member of staff has their own 'Key Children', and we tell you who your baby's Key Worker is.

You get information every day about feeds and nappy changes, and anything else important is talked through with you at pick-up.

  • 1 adult to 3 babies
  • A named Key Worker
  • Daily feed and nappy notes
  • Play based on the senses

02

Young Preschooler

First words, first stories

Circle timeevery day, to get children talking

Children in this group are busy being creative, using their imagination to discover things and solve problems. Language grows fast through role play and shared reading.

In daily circle time we encourage children to talk more, and many start putting sentences together. 'Open expression' lets them explore all kinds of materials, building the small-muscle skills they'll need for writing.

Staff help with eating, dressing and toileting so independence can grow, and we praise every new achievement to build self-esteem.

  • Role play
  • Shared reading
  • Daily circle time
  • Eating, dressing, toileting

03

Toddlers

Sharing and playing together

Joint playsharing, taking turns, cause and effect

When children are ready for the next step, skills develop quickly. They use what they learned earlier to come up with new ideas and new ways to tackle problems.

We encourage joint play: inventing scenarios, using their imagination in games, and finding out about the wider world. This is where they learn to share and cooperate.

Through carefully planned activities with age-appropriate resources, we build communication, social, thinking and self-help skills, and start on literacy, maths and communication in preparation for school.

  • Joint play
  • Cause and effect
  • Sharing and cooperating
  • Early literacy and maths

04

Preschooler

Getting ready for school

PhonicsLetters and Sounds, then Jolly Phonics in the last term

Our Pre-school class focuses on independence and problem solving, and on the social and personal skills children need to cope well with change, challenge, conflict and choice.

Children learn and play in small groups. Role play, construction and games teach sharing and cooperating, and a wider range of equipment and materials helps them understand cause and effect.

Literacy and numeracy grow fast at this age. Phase One of 'Letters and Sounds' is introduced in class, and 'Jolly Phonics' is phased in during the last term, or when the children are ready.

  • Letters and Sounds
  • Jolly Phonics
  • Small-group learning
  • Ready for school

The British EYFS, seven areas

We follow the Early Years Foundation Stage framework, and implement the EYFS 2021. Progress is assessed with the EYFS Profile, and every area of learning is planned through play.

Prime areasthe three that spark curiosity and enthusiasm

Communication and Language
Uses words and gestures to talk with others; enjoys books and makes up stories; makes marks and starts to give them meaning; starts to link sounds and letters.
Physical Development
Running, jumping and climbing with the large muscles; sewing, threading, cutting, painting, drawing and tearing; learns about their health and safety.
Personal, Social and Emotional
Shares and takes turns with others; enjoys the company of friends; learns to behave responsibly and deal with feelings; starts to care for themselves.

Specific areaswhere the prime areas are strengthened and used

Literacy
Links sounds and letters; begins to read and write; has a wide range of books to spark their interest.
Mathematics
Joins in with singing and rhymes; explores patterns and shapes in play; uses measures in cooking or water play; jigsaws, matching games and puzzles.
Understanding the World
Finds out about the world; uses ICT: computers, telephones, tills; understands the passing of time; takes part in multicultural activities.
Expressive Arts and Design
Play dough, modelling, drawing, collage, painting and glue; makes music, sings and dances; uses role play to make sense of the world.

Parents as partners

Parents are a child's first and most enduring educators. Your child's Class Teacher and the Nursery Manager are always willing to meet you.

Every day at pick-up
The Class Teacher or Key Worker tells you about your child's day, activities and achievements.
In the first two weeks
You make an appointment with the Class Teacher to talk through your child's needs.
Twice a year
A formal review of progress. You can read your child's records and 'All About Me' folder whenever you wish.
Every month
A newsletter, plus the parents' notice board, keeps you up to date with the nursery.
When you have a concern
If you're worried about your child's development, we work with outside agencies in the UAE and UK to offer support.
