Curriculum

Our teaching follows the National Curriculum for England and Wales, from the Early Years Foundation Stage through Key Stages 1 to 4 and on to AS and A Level in Years 12 and 13. It is adapted to the Kuwait Ministry of Education’s requirements for Arabic language and Islamic Studies.

Many subjects are taught by specialist teachers from Year 1, including P.E., ICT, Computer Studies, Music, French and Art. All teachers are accredited by the Kuwait Ministry of Education for the age groups they teach.

01Lower SchoolKindergarten and Reception. The UK Foundation Stage curriculum.

The Early Years Department has spacious, stimulating classrooms linked to large indoor and outdoor play areas with safe play equipment. There are four Kindergarten rooms and four Reception Year rooms. Independence is encouraged, and each child works towards their potential in:

  • Communication, language and literacy
  • Mathematics
  • Physical development
  • Personal and social development
  • Knowledge and understanding of the world
  • Creative development

Reception children also meet the UK Primary Framework for Literacy and Numeracy. At the end of Reception they sit NFER tests in Literacy and Numeracy.

02Middle SchoolYears 3 to 6. The National Curriculum of England.

The emphasis is on the core subjects: Literacy (especially speaking and listening), reading, Numeracy and Science, with support for children who have English as a second language. In Years 5 and 6 children are set by ability in Literacy and Numeracy.

  • History, Geography, K.S.S. and Art
  • ICT taught by specialist teachers
  • Arabic for Arabs and non-Arabs
  • Islamic Studies for Muslims
  • French in Years 4, 5 and 6
  • P.E. and Music

The school day has 8 lessons of 40 minutes, with two 15-minute breaks at 9.30 and 11.45. Written homework is set every day. Tests are in December and May, reports go home each term, and parents meet every teacher on the termly Open Day.

Call the School OfficeEmail esf@skee.com

03Upper SchoolIGCSE and A Level through the Cambridge International board.

Pupils join the Upper School from age 11 after an entrance examination and an interview. From Year 9 to Year 11 students study up to 10 IGCSE subjects in one-year or two-year programmes, and sit IGCSE examinations in Years 10 and 11.

  • Up to 10 IGCSE subjects
  • Core or Extended papers
  • A/S Level can be sat in May/June of Year 11
  • 2 or 3 A Level subjects can start in Year 11
  • Scripts marked in the UK
  • Cambridge International and Edexcel

For the full list of IGCSE and AS/A2 subjects, please contact the Head of Upper School.

Ask for the Head of Upper School

04Sixth Form CentreYears 12 and 13, preparing for university.

The Sixth Form Centre gives Years 12 and 13 their own study space, with wireless internet and computers. Students set their own rules for using it, and learn to manage their time the way they will need to at university.

Almost all universities, especially in Medicine and Engineering, look for results above IGCSE. A/S Level counts as half an A Level at universities and colleges worldwide.

Call about Sixth Form places

05Arabic and Islamic StudiesTo the Kuwait Ministry of Education syllabus.

Arabic language and Islamic Studies are taught for both Arabs and non-Arabs, in separate classes, following the regulations of the Private Education Department. Extra support is planned with the parents when a pupil needs it.

  • Kuwaiti High School Secondary Examination
  • IGCSE Arabic as a First Language

Call the Arabic Department

06FrenchYears 4 to 9, then an option in Years 10 to 12.

Every student learns French from Year 4 to Year 9, with course materials matched to each level. It continues as an optional subject in Years 10 to 12.

07Assessment and reportsHow parents follow their child’s progress.
  • Baseline assessment in the first month of Early Years
  • NFER tests at the end of Reception
  • Mid-year and end-of-year tests in KS2
  • CAT3 assessments at the end of Year 6
  • Assessment profile through Secondary
  • An Open Day for parents each term

Teachers are glad to talk with parents at any time, by arrangement with the School Office, in the consultation room set aside for private discussions.

08Clubs and activitiesRun by our staff during and after school hours.
  • Under-14 and Under-16 football
  • Under-16 girls’ football
  • Volleyball, rounders, table tennis
  • Swimming for girls and for boys
  • Karate, badminton, boxing
  • Choir and the ESF Band
  • Painting, drawing and ceramics
  • Debating and chess
  • Ice-skating for beginners
  • Creative embroidery

Forms go home at the start of each term’s programme and places are first come, first served. Students take part in inter-school events through KFSAC and ISACK, the annual Charity Fayre, Book Week, International Day, plays and concerts.

09Health, uniform and resourcesThe practical things parents ask about.

School nurse. A full-time, qualified nurse works in the ground-floor clinic and stays until every student has left, including after activities. She keeps a health record for each pupil, with check-ups under Ministry of Health rules.

Uniform. Compulsory, imported from the U.K., and sold at the Uniform Shop opposite the Cashier’s office.

Resources. A fully equipped resource unit with teaching materials imported from the U.K.
