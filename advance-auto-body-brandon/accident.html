Had an accident?

Being involved in a collision can be a scary experience for anyone. Here's a step-by-step guide to follow. When everyone is safe, call us on 204-727-8585 and we'll take it from there.

Step by step

Keep this page on your phone. Most of all, remain calm.

Call Advance →

  1. Stop.

    If your vehicle is involved in an accident and you don't stop, you may be subject to criminal prosecution.

  2. Call 911 when you need to.

    If anyone is injured, if the total damage to all vehicles appears to be more than $1,000, or if you suspect another driver of a Criminal Code offence (such as driving under the influence), call 911 and follow the operator's instructions. Do not try to move anyone injured. If no one is hurt and the damage looks to be less than $1,000, call your local police for instructions. If police don't attend, they'll tell you to report the collision to Manitoba Public Insurance within 24 hours.

  3. Move out of traffic if it's safe.

    If your vehicle can't be driven, turn on your hazard lights or use cones, warning triangles or flares. If you have a camera or phone, take pictures of the scene, ideally before the vehicles are moved, and use your phone to audio record as much information as you can.

  4. Write down the other drivers' details.

    Names, addresses, phone and driver's licence numbers of all other drivers, the licence plates of the other vehicles, the names and addresses of the registered owners, and the insurance information for each vehicle.

  5. Get passengers' and witnesses' details.

    Names, addresses and phone numbers.

  6. Note the scene.

    Jot down specific details about the scene using the accident report sheet.

  7. Report the incident to Manitoba Public Insurance.

    Then call us about the repair: 204-727-8585.

Remember

  • Stay calm, as difficult as it may seem.
  • Don't argue with other drivers and passengers. Save your story for the police.
  • Don't take responsibility, sign statements about fault, or promise to pay for damage at the scene.
  • Watch for unauthorized tow truck operators pushing you to tow, demanding immediate payment, or steering you to a shop of their choice. If you feel pressured, ask the police for an authorized operator and have the car taken to a Collision Reporting Centre or police compound until you talk to your insurer.

Write down

  • Other driver: name, address, day and night phone, licence number and expiry
  • Their vehicle: year, make and model, owner if not the driver
  • Their insurance company, policy number and expiry
  • What happened, the damage to vehicles and property, and any injuries
  • A sketch of the scene: direction of each vehicle and point of contact
  • Witnesses: names, addresses and phone numbers

Tell police within 7 days if

  • A fatality
  • A serious injury (someone hospitalized)
  • An unlicensed driver or unregistered vehicle
  • You couldn't get the other party's particulars
  • Suspected drug or alcohol use by the other driver

Keep in your trunk

  • A basic first aid kit
  • Emergency road flares, warning triangles or cones
  • A fire extinguisher (A-B-C type)
  • A flashlight and extra batteries
  • Bottled water
  • Booster cables
  • A tire repair kit and pump
  • A small tool kit
  • A towel
  • A pair of work gloves
  • Some nonperishable food
  • Hand wipes
  • A thermal blanket
